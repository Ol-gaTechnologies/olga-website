Before you go

Choose the room by who is in it.

There are four things on next month’s calendar and you can justify one. Ol-ga answers the only question that decides it: which of them is holding people whose intent lines up with yours — buyers for what you sell, suppliers for what you need, capital for what you are building.

Go where the match is

Events, venues and workspaces list on Ol-ga. You see where the other side of your sentence will be standing, then register or book through us.

Meet on purpose

A short, explained list of people worth meeting — three or four, not three hundred. Both of you have to say yes before either name is shared.

Skip the warm-up

You already know what they are looking for and they know what you do, so the conversation starts at the point most first meetings never reach.

After you leave

The value of a room is decided months after everybody has gone home.

Most networking ends at the exit. Ol-ga keeps the part that matters: who you met, what they were looking for in their own words, what you said you would do, and whether it happened.

  • Your contacts update themselves. When someone changes what they offer or what they need, their entry changes with it — you are never working from a card printed two years ago.
  • You are told when something in your own network becomes relevant. Not a feed — a small number of things actually worth your attention.
  • Every conversation has an endpoint you can reopen: pick it up in eighteen months with full clarity on where it was left.

Two years in

Five thousand people you have actually met.

Not connections. People you stood in front of, spoke to, and agreed to keep. On the day you start something new, you ask Ol-ga who among them is right for it — and you get a ranked answer, with the reason attached. That is the asset we are building for you. Everything before it is groundwork.
